# C02 · Proposal & Eval Plan (GATE)

**Meetings:** D87–D88 · **Points:** 15 pts

**Video/Source Link(s):** None. Reference your own A12 eval work. You already know how to do this.

**Notes**  
**The eval plan is written before any code.** That is not a stylistic preference, it is the gate. I will not sign off on a proposal without a defined success metric, and without sign-off you do not proceed.

If you cannot say how you will know whether it worked, you do not yet have a project, you have an intention.

Your risk register should name the thing most likely to kill this. Be specific. "The API might be slow" is not a risk. "The document corpus I'm planning on is 400 PDFs of scanned handwriting and I have not verified that OCR works on them at all" is a risk, and it's the one that will actually end your project.

Scope down. Every capstone in the history of this kind of course was too ambitious in week 1. The projects that finish are the ones that were cut in half at the proposal stage.

**Deliverable**  
`PROPOSAL.md`: problem statement · success metric with a baseline measurement · eval plan (what you'll measure, on what data, how often) · division of labor if pairing · risk register with your top three risks and a mitigation for each · a week-by-week plan through the symposium on Thu May 13.

**Reflection Questions**

1. State your success metric in one sentence, precisely enough that someone else could compute it.
2. What is your baseline number for that metric, measured, not estimated? How did you measure it?
3. What data will you evaluate on, how many examples, and where does the ground truth come from?
4. What is your top risk, and what is the earliest week you would know it had materialized?
5. What did you cut from your original idea, and what made you cut it?
6. What is the smallest version of this that would still be worth demoing? Describe it in two sentences.
7. Which week in your plan has the most work in it? What happens to the schedule if that week goes badly?
8. If pairing: what exactly does each partner own, and what is the interface between your two pieces?
