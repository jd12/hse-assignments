**Meetings:** D98–D100 · **Points:** 15 pts

**Video/Source Link(s):** None.

**Notes**  
Last week new functionality may be added. After Friday, no new features: only fixes, evals, and documentation.

Do the eval run now, not in Week 32. If your numbers are bad, this is the last week you can do anything about it.

Start the technical write-up this week even though it isn't due yet. Writing it will find bugs. It always does: explaining why the system behaves as it does forces you to check whether it actually does.

**Deliverable**  
Sprint 4 commits + a full eval run with numbers + `TECHNICAL.md` started: the headings filled in with bullets and numbers, no prose sections.

**Discussion · Fri Apr 30, in class · technical defense, round one**  
Twelve minutes each, and this is the rehearsal for the real one at freeze. Questions below so you can prepare. Ungraded, and nothing is handed in for it.

1. What does your system actually do, mechanically, in the order it does it?
2. Name the one design decision the whole project rests on. What was the alternative, and why did you rule it out?
3. Where does your system get its answer wrong, and what causes it?
4. What is your metric, what is the baseline, and what is the number today?
5. If I swapped your implementation for a completely different one, which of your claims would still be true? Those are the claims that are not about your system.

**Reflection Questions**

1. What is your metric now, over how many runs, and what is the spread?
2. Is your improvement over baseline larger than your run-to-run variance? Show the numbers.
3. What did filling in the `TECHNICAL.md` headings make you go back and check in the code?
4. What is the last feature you added, and was it worth the time?
5. What is going to be embarrassing at the symposium, and can you fix it in one week?
